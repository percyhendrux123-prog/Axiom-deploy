Fields

Who it’s for

The job changes by field. The rule doesn’t: anything that sends, spends or commits the business waits for your yes.

Insurance agencies

Client care after a policy issues: a birthday note where there is a date, then regular check-ins by text and email through the first year, each one checked before it goes for opt-out, the client’s own local hours and the approved wording. See the field report

Law offices

The attorney’s calendar and deadlines kept straight, and routine letters and record requests drafted for review before they go.

Financial advisors and CPAs

Client review meetings scheduled on their cycle, open paperwork tracked, and the follow-up after each meeting drafted.

Real estate teams

Past-client care after closing: anniversary notes, check-ins and review requests at set points, with the client records kept current.

Medical and dental practices

Appointment changes, reminders and patient callbacks drafted from your own systems for your staff to approve.

Coaches and fitness studios

Member follow-up: check-ins, renewals and the members who have gone quiet, each message written in your words.

Founders’ offices

Calendar, inbox triage, meeting prep and the follow-ups that come out of each meeting.

The name

Built on an axiom

An axiom is a first truth. It is not proved; everything else is built on it.

Euclid’s Elements opens with axioms: five postulates and five common notions. Its first proposition builds an equilateral triangle from two circles.

Given a straight line AB:

  1. 1With center A, draw the circle through B.
  2. 2With center B, draw the circle through A.
  3. 3Where the circles cut, mark C.
  4. 4Join CA and CB. Each is a radius equal to AB, so all three sides are equal.

Our mark is that triangle, left undrawn: three cut circles, and the eye completes a shape nobody drew. Gaetano Kanizsa described the effect in 1955.

Axiom is built the same way. One rule comes first, and every agent we deploy works from it.

Specialists do the assigned work and return evidence up the chain. Saying “done” is not a check.

World / outside the session

A message to another person leaves the session. The outbound edge is where the advisory card and send checks sit.

Refusal log / a visible stop

A refused action leaves a record instead of disappearing. Tripwire watches tool refusals, not just outbound sends.

axioma / a second look

Before a consequential call, Axiōma presents an advisory card. It advises; it never blocks or grants permission.

The spec is CC0 and the code is MIT.

gatekeeper / before a send

Checks supported terminal sends for consent, suppression, quiet hours and repeat messages. A failed check blocks that send.

standing / check the evidence

A worker must run a passing proof check before marking its task done. Standing checks the file, page or allowed command, not the claim.

tripwire / record a refusal

Logs a refused tool action and alerts the owner. It records the stop; it does not grant an exception.

handydandy / leave a handoff

Notices when a session runs long and prompts a written handoff for the next session.

The stop

Where an agent stops and asks

drag the medallion · the words make room · arrow keys work too

the marked phrases are lit; the key below explains each color

Around 520 BC, Hipparchus of Athens set stone herms along the roads and carved short maxims into them, so anyone walking past could read the law on the messenger's stone. Walk with just intent. Do not deceive a friend.

We run our own two companies on the same idea. Sixteen specialist AI agents (as of Nov 9) (second look) work behind one front door, and a written permission gate decides what each one may do alone. Money, new prices and first contact with strangers wait for a person.

An agent can build a payment link and check that the page opens. It never presses pay: completing a live payment (STOP) is reserved to the owner, so the agent stops there and reports exactly what is ready.

Every message is reserved before it sends, because a worker that crashes and retries could text the same person twice (CAUTION). We would rather miss one message and see it in the log than send it twice. The loop that answers when an agent gets stuck is public code, with 9 passing tests (GO).

Those colors are Axiōma, the signal our agents use when they write to a person. Red is stop, yellow is caution, green is a check that really passed, and pale gold is a whisper. The signal advises. The gate stays with you. Axiōma is given away: the spec is CC0 and the code is MIT.

Each marked phrase carries its Axiōma signal color and a second cue besides color:

  • red, bold, solid line: stop. Do not proceed until it is fixed.
  • yellow, wavy line: caution. It works, but it starts something that cuts against your interest.
  • green, dashed line: go. A real check passed.
  • pale gold, dotted line: whisper. A second look; it stops nothing.

Process

Method

When rules and regulations begin to move, it's no longer solely governance. It's operations.

Before a system runs, we write down what starts the work, what counts as done, and where it has to stop and ask.

  1. 01Intake.A request, message or deadline enters the system.
  2. 02Context.Prior notes and decisions are gathered first, so the owner is not asked to explain again.
  3. 03Preparation.The draft, the edit or the next action is produced in full.
  4. 04Decision.Anything that commits the business waits for the owner.
  5. 05Record.The outcome is checked and written down, so the next session starts where this one ended.
Read the transcript

Operations brief. One client, starting from zero. Every mechanism we run, in the order the work actually happens. A box is a step the system runs. A diamond is a check that runs every time. A square is a person saying yes.

It starts with research. The business, its market, its audience, and the facts. The owner gets a short yes-or-no list. Nothing is posted yet.

Then content. The owner films on their own phone. We edit, keep their exact words, and write the captions. It’s checked for facts and fit, and the owner approves it before it goes public. Engagement turns into new prospects, and what worked feeds the next round of research.

New messages are checked every five minutes, and the owner gets a private alert. A reply is drafted in their words. It only goes out when the owner says yes. Money, contracts and complaints always go to a person.

Every call is answered. The caller is told it’s an AI. Spam is screened, the details are taken, and a person will take over the call. We run this on our own business first.

After the sale, no one is forgotten. The day a policy is issued, the client’s first year is planned. Check-ins all year, with consent, opt-outs and quiet hours respected. A referral ask only goes out with the owner’s yes.

The owner has one thread with us, and every message is read and logged. Simple questions get answered. New work or money goes to a person. Anything personal is left alone.

Every mechanism passes the same gates. Facts checked against sources. Fit with the client. A fresh set of eyes. Then a person says yes, and it’s logged.

Put together, that’s one client’s full system. Research feeds content. Content brings prospects. Prospects become clients. Clients are cared for, and what’s learned goes back to the start. Now zoom out. Same system, many clients. Nothing in your business waits on you to remember it. Axiom.
