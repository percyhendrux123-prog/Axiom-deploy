An independent insurance agency: from no contact after the sale to live client care in 7 days

The owner sold policies, then had no contact with clients for the next year. The agency's agents had the same gap, and no one on staff could do the follow-up. We built a system that runs the contact after the sale under the owner's approval rules, then hands any reply to a person.

How it is governed

  • Every send passes a check first: consent on file, opt-out, quiet hours, holidays, licensed state, no repeats.
  • A send that fails a check is held with a written reason and reported, not sent.
  • Automatic texts stop the moment a client replies. The owner or the agent takes over.
  • A read-only check runs every morning and flags anything that should have sent but did not.

What broke, and how it recovered

On November 17 the owner's computer lost name lookups for about three and a half hours. The daily check flagged 8 texts as unconfirmed. Once the network came back, the same check read 9 of 9 delivered. The alarms were the check failing to look up status, not failed texts.

At the 9:15 AM check, 7 of the 61 entries had no first contact yet. Six were held with a reason on record: an agent not yet switched on, a state license not confirmed, a missing time zone. One was inside its normal sending window. Two removal requests were marked pending and being checked. Nothing is sent around a hold.

The escalation desk: when an agent gets stuck, someone answers

Each client has an agent on the client's own computer. When that agent meets something it cannot handle, it has to reach someone, even at night with no chat window open.

The loop catches the escalation, sends the client one holding line, opens one ticket and hands it to a worker. Every action the fix wants to take is checked against a permission gate. Money, deletes, new prices, strangers and production changes wait for a person, and so does anything the gate does not recognize. Every send is reserved first, so a retry cannot text the client twice.

One real evening, October 29, 2026: a client's agent held a launch post because it could not see a chat window while the client was on a video call. The ticket opened at 6:35 PM and closed at 6:39 PM. Nothing was broken; the window was closed. The worker did not reopen it, because it could have appeared on a shared screen. One reply told the client what to click after the call.

The code, the tests and a replay of that evening: github.com/percyhendrux123-prog/escalation-desk.

A wellness business: one person's content team

The founder had hours of customer testimonial footage and no time to turn it into posts.

The system finds the clean cut points in the footage and drafts the captions. A second model reviews each caption against the founder's list of words to avoid. Every choice is logged with its reason, so the founder can read why a line was written the way it was.

The retainer is the only revenue stated on this page, and the only figure here we can show a payment record for. Hours saved and post counts are the founder's numbers to give. They are blank until then.

Forward-deployed: a small contractor's first large-project work

A small trades contractor (lawn care, painting, concrete) wants steady work from the large projects coming to the state, through bids and vendor registrations, not ads.

We went into the business first. We read the public records and registrations and mapped what has to be in place before a bid. Then we scanned public bid boards. On November 18, 2026 there were 12 live listings in the contractor's trades, 7 of them the contractor can pursue: 3 as direct bids and 4 as a subcontractor.
