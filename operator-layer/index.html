The operator layer

What happens when a small team's AI agent hits a failure.

How it works

  1. The agent escalates in one line: what failed, and where.
  2. The founder gets a holding reply.
  3. The operator finds the cause and backs up what it's about to change.
  4. The operator runs the job again. A fix is a claim until it passes.
  5. A rule goes in so a repeat gets caught.
  6. The founder gets a plain report: what broke, what changed, what's next.

Where it stops

  • The loop starts when a failure is noticed. Failures nobody notices need their own checks; we don't claim the loop catches them.
  • A re-run proves the job completes. It doesn't prove every output is right.
  • One founder-approval question is a third contact; "hears twice" is the normal path, not a promise.

The operator is Axiom: AI agents under one accountable human, working with the access the founder granted, inside the founder's rules. It does not decide money, prices or promises.

What stays with the founder

By rule, the founder decides anything involving money, prices or promises, and the operator asks rather than acts on those.

What the record holds

Decisions and task events are written down as they happen.

Why small teams

Funded teams are getting smaller. Carta's State of Startup Compensation, H2 2025, puts the median seed-stage team at four employees, and average Series B headcount fell from 53 in 2023 to 45 in 2025. Source: carta.com/data/startup-compensation-h2-2025

A smaller team leaves fewer people to notice when an agent fails, so the path a failure takes has to be decided ahead of time. The loop above has the same shape as what Google's SRE book calls a postmortem: a written record, the cause, and the follow-up that keeps it from happening again. Source: sre.google/sre-book/postmortem-culture
